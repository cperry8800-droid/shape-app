## What was built

**First pass:**
- The Club Shape venue.
- Two media players and a mixer, modelled on the CDJ-3000 / DJM-A9 layout with no trademarks on
  screen.
- A camera director: panoramic, decks, mixer, face and shoulder shots, cut on the bar.
- A two-deck mix planner that cues, blends and swaps on bar lines.
- A labelled synthesized example set, and "Next track ⇄".

**Second pass** (owner: *"make her movements more fluid, relaxed, and also make the crowd have the
same avatar design. Not look like stick figures"*):
- Nora's hands move on speed- and acceleration-limited springs. Measured peaks are 1.42 m/s and
  21.8 m/s², down from 34 m/s².
- A skin shader ceiling stops her hands blowing out to white.
- The crowd is baked from her own VRM: three hair lengths, a tint per person and a shader arm
  raise. It draws 697 people in 8 draw calls.

## The open question: "how do we improve the graphics?"

The owner asked this and it has not been built. Here is the plan, from what the code does today.
Today's setup:
- ACES tone mapping at exposure 1.05.
- `UnrealBloomPass` at 256×256 with threshold 0.82.
- One `FogExp2` haze.
- A small PMREM environment.
- A key spot, a top spot, a wall `RectAreaLight`, a hemisphere light, and additive beam cones.
- No shadows.

**First batch (biggest visible change for the least cost; it costs phones nothing):**

1. **Relight and change the tone mapping.** Try `AgXToneMapping` or `NeutralToneMapping` in place of
   ACES. Rebuild the rig as a key, a fill and two coloured rims, plus a back light on the crowd. The
   rear balcony reads black from behind today. Once her skin is lit properly, the skin ceiling hack
   in `noraPerformer.mjs` can go. It is a clamp covering for the lighting.
2. **Bloom resolution.** 256×256 gives soft, blocky halos. Run it at half the screen size on the
   high tier and keep 256 on the low tier.
3. **Materials.**
   - Gear: brushed-metal jog rims, glossy plastic, and screens that glow with a faint glass
     reflection off the environment map.
   - Nora: tune her MToon shade colour and rim so she stops reading as an avatar under stage light.

**Second batch:**

4. **Grounding.**
   - One shadow-casting key light (a 1024 map, Nora and the gear only).
   - Baked ambient occlusion on the booth and floor.
   - Blob shadows under the crowd.

   Today everything floats.
5. **Atmosphere.**
   - Volumetric beams: noise-textured cones and light shafts through the haze.
   - Floating dust.
   - Beams timed to the beat.

   This costs fill rate, so it is high tier only.
6. **Crowd.**
   - A finer hair mesh on the near LOD: it reads as a helmet at close range today.
   - Hair colour variation.
   - Rim light on shoulders.

**Third batch:**

7. **The LED wall:** a beat-synced shader in place of the static content.
8. **Camera craft:** depth of field on the close shots only, slight handheld drift, grain and
   vignette.
9. **The real Nora VRM** in place of `placeholder.vrm`. This is the owner's asset, and it is the
   biggest single change to how she looks.

**Measure before and after each batch.**
- Draw calls and triangles: `glinfo.cjs`.
- Motion: `motion-sample.cjs`.
- Screenshots: `shot3.cjs`.
- The phone layout: `phone-run.cjs`.

Today's budget is 1.75M triangles on the high tier and 0.93M on the phone tier. Every check here is
headless SwiftShader at 1–3 fps, so **a real mid-range phone has never run this.** That test is owed
before any of it goes into the app. Republish to the same artifact URL after each batch.
