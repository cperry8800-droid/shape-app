Shape, with depth.

A review of the website and the three dashboards, and what a depth pass would do to them. Every "after" below is a screenshot of the proposed styling. Nothing has been changed in the repo or on the live site.

01 · Diagnosis

Why it reads flat

The brand, the photography and the type are strong. What is missing is light: almost every surface is drawn as an outline on a background of nearly the same value, so nothing stands off the page.

Dashboards

Cards are a hairline, not an object

A white card on the #f4f6f5 ground with a 0 1px 2px shadow at 4% opacity. The two surfaces differ by about 3% in brightness, so the 1px border is the only thing drawing the card.

Dashboards

The grid clips every shadow

Each grid cell is overflow: hidden (its height measurement needs it), so even a strong shadow on a card is cut off at the card's edge. The cell itself has to cast the shadow.

Website

Dark cards on a dark page

Cards are a 4% white fill and a 10% white line on near-black, with no shadow and no lit edge. They read as boxes drawn on the page rather than panels above it.

Everywhere

Buttons are flat fills

No highlight, no lip, no shadow. Hover only changes the colour, and a click has no pressed state, so primary actions don't feel pressable.

02 · The proposal

Five lifts, one per job

One depth scale that every surface picks from, defined per colour theme so the dark themes get lit edges where a shadow would not show. Each lift stacks four layers: a lit top edge, a shaded bottom lip, a tight contact shadow and a long soft one.

On top of the lifts: buttons rise 1–2px on hover and press in on click; showcase cards on the website lean up to 5° toward the pointer with a soft highlight that follows it; the phone mockups get a bezel, a rim light and a slight 3D turn. All motion is off for visitors who ask for reduced motion and on touch screens. Dashboard card colours don't change, so their text contrast doesn't either; on the homepage's lit cards the faintest text still reads 4.9:1 at the brightest point, above the 4.5:1 minimum.

04 · Design and layout findings

What else to fix

Beyond depth, ordered by impact. High means a visitor notices it on first use; low is polish.

Across the site

High

Two type systems, one brand

Homepage, dashboards vs Marketplace, Pricing, Coach, Score, Store, Community, Login

The homepage and dashboards use Anybody, Doto and Schibsted Grotesk. Every other marketing page uses Fraunces serif with outlined italics and Space Grotesk. Clicking from the homepage to Pricing or the Marketplace feels like landing on a different company's site.

Pick one system. The homepage's is newer and matches the app; moving the other pages' headings to it is mostly a font swap in each page's heading styles.

High

Ask Nora and Chat cover content

Every page, bottom right

The two floating pills sit over whatever is in the corner: the Privacy link on Login, the search field on the Store, the demo banner on the phone dashboard.

Reserve 80px of bottom padding on pages that show the pills, or merge them into one button that opens both.

Med

Copy over busy photography

Store hero, Score hero, Community preview banner

On the Store, "Trade Shape Score for Shape merch…" runs across the product flat-lay and its grey text is hard to read on the concrete. The Score subhead crosses the runner.

Add a directional scrim behind the copy column, or move the copy to the photo's empty side.

Low

A coloured emoji in the Community banner

Community · "This is a preview"

The 💬 is the only full-colour emoji on the site, and the house rule is typographic symbols. The banner also takes prime space right under the headline.

Swap for a monochrome mark and move the notice below the first feed item.

Homepage

Med

Coach cards lift on hover but go nowhere

"The marketplace" section

The eight coach cards are plain blocks with a hover lift, which promises a click that doesn't exist.

Make each card a link into the marketplace (filtered to that coach's speciality), or drop the hover lift.

Med

The price section is unbalanced at desktop width

"$5/mo" section, 1440px

The copy sits in a 330px column at the far left and the phone at the far right, with about 600px of empty space between them.

Cap the section at ~1100px and centre it, or use the middle for the three things $5 includes.

Dashboards

Med

The board toolbar outweighs the day

Today, all three roles

Dashboard picker, Save as, Rename, Coaching preset, Undo and Add widget sit above the cards at the same weight as the page's real actions. Customising the board is occasional; it shouldn't be the first row a coach reads.

Fold them into one "Customize" menu; show Undo only after a change.

Med

Card and page are the same value

White theme (the default)

The default theme's ground is so close to white that cards depend entirely on shadow. The lifts fix most of this.

Ship the lifts first; if it still reads flat, take the ground one step darker and re-run the contrast tests for the smallest labels.

Low

The demo band is cut off on a phone

Member Today, 390px

"Preview · demo data — an example of a live account" is clipped at the right edge, and the Nora button sits on top of it.

Let the band wrap to two lines on narrow screens and lift the Nora button above it.

05 · If you want to ship it

What it would take

  1. Dashboards: one stylesheet. The lifts become five tokens in each of the six dashboard themes, applied to cards, grid cells, the side rail, the app bar and buttons. Small, and the existing theme and contrast tests pass with it.
  2. Homepage: one page. Its styles are self-contained, so the keys, lit cards, tilt and phone pose stay inside that file plus a tiny shared pointer-tilt script.
  3. Other marketing pages: per page. They're styled inline, so each showcase card and button needs a class before shared depth styles can reach it. The marketing "afters" above were made by applying those styles at screenshot time.
The depth styling behind these screenshots is saved as a patch in this session, not in the repo. Say the word and it can go up as a pull request for review.
